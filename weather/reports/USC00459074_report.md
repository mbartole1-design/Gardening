# Garden weather report: Wenatchee

Station USC00459074 at 47.429, -120.310, elevation 626 ft. Records since 1931. Unless noted, numbers are based on the last 30 complete years (1996–2025), which reflect today's climate better than the full record. Generated 2026-11-07.

> Weather can't be forecast day-by-day more than about two weeks out. What this report gives you is the odds, based on what actually happened here in recent decades, which is the best guide for a plan a season ahead.

## The short version

- **Last spring frost (32°F):** typically around **Apr 06**. Only a 1-in-10 chance of frost after **Apr 21**. Latest seen: May 02.
- **First fall frost (32°F):** typically around **Oct 29**, with a 1-in-10 chance it comes before **Oct 15**.
- **Frost-free season:** about **204 days** in a typical year.
- **Warm-season crops** (tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, squash) do best once the 7-day average temperature holds above 60°F, typically **May 10** (in a late year May 20).
- **Lettuce and greens window closes** when daytime highs average 80°F+, typically **May 26** (early years May 15). It reopens around **Sep 23**, so sow fall greens from late Aug onward.
- **Heat:** about **40 days ≥ 90°F**, **20 days ≥ 95°F** and **6 days ≥ 100°F** a year. First 90°F day is typically Jun 01.
- **Rain:** about **8.7 in a year**, but only **1.0 in from June through September**. The typical longest dry spell (no day with 0.10 in or more, Apr–Oct) is **106 days**.
- **Water:** plants lose roughly **27 in of water from June through September** (reference evapotranspiration), so almost all of it has to come from irrigation.
- **Wind:** windiest months are Jun, Jul, May (about 10 mph average, with gusts of 35 mph or more on 35 days a year). Wind adds about **8.0 in** (30%) to June–September water loss compared with still air. A windbreak that halves wind speed would save roughly **4.3 in, about 270 gallons per 100 sq ft** over the summer.

"Water loss from wind" is how much more water a fully planted bed loses than it would in near-still air. "Saved by windbreak" is what you would save if a fence, hedge or shade-cloth screen cut the wind in half. A windbreak protects a strip about 5 to 10 times its height downwind, so a 6 ft screen covers 30 to 60 ft. Put it on the side the strong winds come from.

Wind also dries the soil surface and sprinkler spray long before roots get it, so drip or soaker lines under mulch and watering in the early morning, before the afternoon wind picks up, waste far less.

## Heat stress

- Days ≥ 90°F: 40 a year now, vs 32 in 1931-1960.
- Days ≥ 95°F are rising by about 1.1 days per decade.
- Nights that stay ≥ 65°F (these hurt tomato and pepper fruit set): 24 a year.
- First 90°F day: typically Jun 01, early years May 16. Last 90°F day: typically Sep 07.
- Longest run of 95°F+ days in a typical summer: 6 days.
- Hottest day on record: 113°F (2021).

Tomatoes drop blossoms when days run above about 90–95°F with nights above about 70–75°F, so most fruit sets before early July and again from mid-August. Peppers set best from 70–85°F days.

## Fruit trees: chill and winter risk

Chill is counted from Oct 1 to Mar 31. A typical winter here gives **1921 chill hours** (32–45°F; lowest 1473, highest 2410 over 28 winters), 1596 Utah chill units and 102 chill portions. That is far more than any of your trees need, so chill is not the problem here. The real risks are trees finishing their chill early and then blooming into a spring freeze, and hard winter cold.
Chill hours have changed by about +26 hours per decade over the full record.

| Tree | Chill hours needed | Winters with enough chill | Need typically met by | Met by (late winter) | Winters cold enough to injure | Winters cold enough to kill back |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sweet cherry (Bing / Rainier type) | 700–900 | 100% | Dec 05 | Jan 09 | 0 of 28 (≤-10°F) | 0 of 28 (≤-20°F) |
| Italian prune plum (European) | 800–1000 | 100% | Dec 13 | Jan 23 | 0 of 28 (≤-15°F) | 0 of 28 (≤-25°F) |
| Beauty plum (Japanese) | 250–400 | 100% | Nov 04 | Nov 14 | 2 of 28 (≤-5°F) | 0 of 28 (≤-15°F) |
| Fig (hardy type, e.g. Chicago Hardy) | 100–300 | 100% | Oct 20 | Oct 31 | 25 of 28 (≤15°F) | 9 of 28 (≤5°F) |

- **Sweet cherry (Bing / Rainier type):** Blooms early to mid April here. At full bloom, 28°F kills about 10% of flowers and 25°F about 90%.
- **Italian prune plum (European):** Late bloomer, so spring frost is rarely a problem.
- **Beauty plum (Japanese):** Low chill, so it is ready to bloom by midwinter and flowers at the first warm spell. Late-March and April freezes are its main threat.
- **Fig (hardy type, e.g. Chicago Hardy):** Chill is never the issue here; winter cold is. Branches start to die back around 15°F and the top usually dies to the ground near 5°F, though the roots of hardy types regrow.

**Bloom-time freezes:** 32% of springs have at least one night at 28°F or colder between Mar 15 and Apr 30 (about 0.8 such nights a year). The typical coldest winter night is 10°F.
